## 2. Quick Chinese Brief

野生动物交易规模很大——合法的宠物买卖、肉类市场、皮毛和药材，非法的盗猎，全都算在内，大约 **25% 的哺乳动物物种**牵涉其中。科学家一直警告它可能助长**人畜共患病**（病原体从动物跳到人身上引发的疾病，比如 COVID）。

一项发表在《Science》上的新研究把 **40 多年**的交易物种数据和 pathogen 数据集 CLOVER 对比后发现：在 **2,079 种被交易的哺乳动物**里，**41%** 至少和人类共享一种可传播病原体；而**未被交易**的动物里只有 **6.4%**。研究者的结论很有意思：**风险不在于"哪种动物脏"，而在于人类和它们接触的方式**——一个物种在野生动物市场上待满 10 年，就大约多和人类共享一种病原体。

作者强调这不代表每次接触都会引发大流行，但接触和传播的机会增加，就给了病原体变异的机会。所以结论是：需要更好的监测、预测模型和应急预案，并用更严格的监管控制野生动物交易。

## 3. Original Article

### How the wildlife trade boosts the chance of a disease jumping from animals to humans

*Decades of data reveal that animals involved in the wildlife trade—from pet sales to meat markets to illegal poaching—are much more likely to carry pathogens that can infect humans*

![A hand in a blue glove holding a little Brown Myotis bat with its mouth opened.](https://static.scientificamerican.com/dam/asset/f925bb48-716a-4ae4-9691-cd1210884013/Little-Brown-Myotis-bat.jpg?m=1775757158.039&w=600)

*Some commonly traded animals—such as bats—carry pathogens that can not only be transmitted to humans, but have been known to cause outbreaks. ZUMA Press, Inc./Alamy*

The wildlife trade is expansive. Whether legal or not, it encompasses pet sales, meat markets, furs and even medicine. About 25 percent of mammal species are involved in some part of the trade, and scientists have warned that it may contribute to zoonotic diseases—illnesses that arise from pathogens that jump from animals to humans, as had occurred with the virus that causes COVID. But exactly how and how often these diseases are passed from animals to humans isn't always clear.

A new study published today in *Science* reveals a close correlation between species in the wildlife trade and animals that are known to have passed pathogens on to humans.

"There's a strong link," says Jérôme Gippet, an ecologist at the University of Fribourg in Switzerland. "What we could calculate is that, for every 10 years that the species is on the wildlife market, it shares one additional pathogen with humans."

The researchers combed through more than 40 years of data tracing which animal species are involved in the legal or illegal wildlife trades. Then they compared this information with a dataset called CLOVER, which lists the pathogens found in different species. The team's analysis revealed that among 2,079 traded mammal species, 41 percent shared at least one transmissible pathogen with humans. For comparison, just 6.4 percent of nontraded animals shared such a pathogen.

The researchers had expected that humans would be more likely to encounter germs from animals they interacted with closely more often. The study highlights the need for better disease surveillance and outbreak preparation, however, Gippet says.

"Before [the study] my idea of disease transmission risk in wildlife was more like, 'Oh, there's some dirty species,'" he says. "But what we show here is: it's not really about the species; it's more about the humans."

Not all zoonotic pathogens that humans encounter lead to outbreaks or pandemics on the scale of COVID. Some don't harm our species or aren't capable of human-to-human transmission. Still, any increase in exposure and transmission comes with the risk of a virus or bacterium evolving to become something more concerning.

The study "emphasizes the importance of having much better controls and regulation of the wildlife trade," says Wayne Getz, an ecologist at the University of California, Berkeley, who was not involved in the new work.

Preparing for potential disease outbreaks involves increasing global disease surveillance, using predictive models to determine which pathogens should be a research priority and creating action plans for when potential outbreaks are detected. The study authors hope their research inspires more preparation.

"There's no safe trade," Gippet says. "Trade, in itself, creates the opportunity for pathogen transmission."

## 7. Sentence Patterns

**① Whether legal or not, it encompasses A, B and even C.**
> Whether legal or not, it encompasses pet sales, meat markets, furs and even medicine.
> 替换练习：*Whether planned or not, it covers hiring, training and even office space.*

**② But exactly how and how often ... isn't always clear.**
> But exactly how and how often these diseases are passed from animals to humans isn't always clear.
> 替换练习：*But exactly how and how often these bugs reach production isn't always clear.*

**③ What we could calculate is that ...**
> What we could calculate is that, for every 10 years on the market, the species shares one additional pathogen with humans.
> 替换练习：*What we could measure is that, for every extra shard, write throughput goes up.*

**④ ... , which lists the ... found in different species.**
> They compared this information with a dataset called CLOVER, which lists the pathogens found in different species.
> 替换练习：*We compared it with a dashboard, which lists every failed request in the last hour.*

**⑤ For comparison, just X percent of ...**
> For comparison, just 6.4 percent of nontraded animals shared such a pathogen.
> 替换练习：*For comparison, just 3 percent of users hit that path.*

**⑥ Still, any increase in ... comes with the risk of ...**
> Still, any increase in exposure and transmission comes with the risk of a virus evolving to become something more concerning.
> 替换练习：*Still, any increase in traffic comes with the risk of the database becoming a bottleneck.*

**⑦ Preparing for ... involves A, B and C.**
> Preparing for potential disease outbreaks involves increasing surveillance, using predictive models and creating action plans.
> 替换练习：*Preparing for an incident involves monitoring, alerting and a written action plan.*

## 8. Reading Comprehension

1. What exactly does the wildlife trade include?
   **Answer:** Both legal and illegal parts — pet sales, meat markets, furs, medicine and illegal poaching.

2. What is a zoonotic disease?
   **Answer:** An illness that arises from pathogens jumping from animals to humans, as happened with the virus that causes COVID.

3. What two datasets did the researchers compare?
   **Answer:** Over 40 years of data on which species are involved in legal or illegal wildlife trade, and the CLOVER dataset, which lists pathogens found in different species.

4. What were the key numbers in the study?
   **Answer:** Among 2,079 traded mammal species, 41 percent shared at least one transmissible pathogen with humans; for comparison, only 6.4 percent of nontraded animals did.

5. What did Gippet say the finding changes about how we think about risk?
   **Answer:** He said it is not really about the species being "dirty" — it is more about the humans, because trade creates the opportunity for transmission.

6. Does every zoonotic pathogen cause a pandemic?
   **Answer:** No. Some do not harm humans or cannot be transmitted between humans, but more exposure and transmission raise the risk of a pathogen evolving into something more concerning.

7. What do the authors and outside experts want to happen next?
   **Answer:** Better controls and regulation of the wildlife trade, more global disease surveillance, predictive models for research priorities, and action plans for outbreaks.
